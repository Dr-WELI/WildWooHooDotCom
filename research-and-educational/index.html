Research and educational

Problem

Research lives where most people never look. The question is where, and on whose terms, people meet it. We bring behavioural-ecology research to the cultural spaces where attention already sits.

Jump toResearchProgrammesScopeEngagementAcademicPartner

Approach

People meet science on their own ground, not only in journals. Songs, screens, classrooms and festivals can carry research into everyday culture. We work in those spaces, and we keep the research accurate as it moves.

Kangaroo Time is the worked example. A peer-reviewed kangaroo study became a song and film. It reached audiences well beyond the original paper. We describe it in full in the Dance Your PhD article.

Research

Some research feeds our educational work. Some stays academic. We currently work on three lines, led by Dr WELI.

Behavioural ecology

We study animal behaviour through an evolutionary and ecological lens. This covers individual personality, social environment and maternal effects across mammals. It is anchored in Dr WELI's PhD on eastern grey kangaroos at ANU. The work continues through collaborations across macropod and ungulate research networks.

Science in Music and Pop Culture

This is an academic line. It runs with Dr Anna-Sophie Jurgens, through an ANU CPAS Visiting Fellowship. We are working on two writing projects. The first treats music as science communication, with Kangaroo Time as the case study. The second is on camp science, and what alien cinema contributes to science communication. Not all of this becomes audience-facing material. The full research profile sits at drweli.com/academic.

Future interests

Our forward interests sit at the intersection of behavioural ecology and cultural cognition. We ask what an evolved primate brain does in a world it was not shaped for. We also ask what popular culture does with that mismatch.

  • Human-animal cognition and social-system parallels. Personality, rank, group decisions and learning read across species. We treat them as comparative biology, not anthropomorphism.
  • Cultural cognition and routine thinking. Thought templates are transmitted culturally and learned young. They shape biases about longevity, evolution and the species we share the planet with.
  • Collective imaginaries and emotion in music. We study how music stages our search for meaning. We ask how that shapes our bond with nature.
  • The evolved brain in synthetic environments. The human brain evolved over many thousands of years. It now runs in a screen-mediated world barely 15 years old. We ask what that does to attention, mental health and child development.

For HDR supervisors and student applicants. We are open to research partnerships and co-supervision conversations. Send a research enquiry.

Programmes

Young audiences rarely meet current research in a form they can move to. Kangaroo Time Kids puts kangaroo behaviour into music, dance and embodied learning. It is the kids version of Kangaroo Time, and it is in delivery now.

We deliver it through Dr WELI's schools engagement programme. Formats include assemblies, STEAM classroom sessions and festival keynotes, for primary and lower-secondary audiences.

Scope

Schools and buyers need to know what is coming, not only what exists. Nothing in this list is shipping yet. We name the scope here so partners can plan with us.

  • Children's books. Illustrated story collections grounded in behavioural-ecology research.
  • Classroom and school materials. Educator-facing kits and lesson formats built around how children learn.
  • Merch. Character and theme-based goods through our shops.
  • Themed social-media content. Short-form material organised around our current research themes.
  • Animation and television. A children's animated series, if a writer-producer partner comes on board.

Engagement

Live engagement is where research meets a room in person. We have delivered workshops, talks and festival programmes over several years. The events below are a sample.

  • Floriade 2025, Canberra. Performance, workshop and Q&A at Commonwealth Park, on the Science and Nature theme.
  • ACT National Science Week, 2024 and 2025. Programme delivery with Inspiring Australia ACT.
  • Encontro Anual de Etologia 2024, Brazil. Commissioned opening plenary, Cabra da Peste.
  • ANU HDR Research Festival. Featured contribution.
  • ANU Three Minute Thesis 2024. Halftime feature.
  • ANU Commencement 2025. Co-master of ceremonies.
  • Athens Sci-Co. International science-communication appearance.
  • SCOM6006, ANU. Guest lecture at the Centre for the Public Awareness of Science.

For engagement enquiries about workshops, talks and school visits, see the Dr WELI programme pages.

Academic

Public-facing work reads better when the research behind it is sound. Our practice is anchored in a peer-reviewed kangaroo study. It covers personality, social environment and maternal effects. You can read the 2023 paper in Behavioral Ecology.

Dr WELI holds an ANU PhD in ecology. He is also an ANU CPAS Visiting Fellow. The full academic profile lives at drweli.com/academic.

Partner

Good educational work needs partners who can carry it into schools and homes. We are open to organisations working on children's media, science engagement and evidence-led education.

  • Publishers and broadcasters. Children's media, illustrated books, animated series and school formats.
  • Universities and research partners. Co-supervision, research collaboration and public engagement programmes.
  • Schools, museums and science centres. Programming and live engagement formats grounded in current research.
  • Cultural and arts partners. Festivals, residencies and place-based collaborations.

If that sounds like your organisation, tell us what you do.
