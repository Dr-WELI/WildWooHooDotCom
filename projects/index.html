Project 01 · Active Flagship · Ever-growing album · 2026–

Why We Smile

An ever-growing album about our ever-changing world — a project that, like a kangaroo, keeps growing for life

Photo by Weliton Menário Costa.

wildwoohoo.com/projects/#why-we-smile → Collaborate with us

Concept developed: since 2025 · Launching: 2026–2027 (Phase 1, three songs) · Format: Three new songs every year, indefinitely · © Weliton Menário Costa / WildWooHoo. All rights reserved.

Concept, aim, energy & how it starts

A spark of hope. A joyful madness. A celebration of us and nature as one.

What it is. Why We Smile is WildWooHoo Studio's flagship music project — an ever-growing album, inspired by the indeterminate growth of kangaroos, our creative director's PhD study organism. The concept touches global issues, the climate crisis among them, but at its heart it is a celebration of us and nature as a unity. A living album that keeps growing — the way kangaroos do.

The aim. The album carries the energy of big championships — the moments when the incredible skills of humans are put to the test, the world watches, and there is a winner. We want us to be the winner in a world that understands that without nature there is none of us. Music as the room where this gets sung and felt, not argued.

The format — indeterminate growth, inspired by kangaroos.

Most albums ship and stop. Why We Smile doesn't.

Kangaroos have indeterminate growth — their skeletons keep growing throughout their lives (so they don't just get fatter, they get bigger). It's a trait found in plenty of animals, but rare for mammals — and one of the most remarkable facts about kangaroos.

Why We Smile borrows that pattern. Three songs in year one. Three in year two. Three in year three. Then at least one new song every year after that, for as long as the album keeps living. The shape mirrors a kangaroo's life: faster growth at the start, then slower but consistent — for life. In practice, it is a three-year album, and then an annual living catalogue.

The format draws on the macropod life-history research of Marco Festa-Bianchet (Université de Sherbrooke) and the kangaroo-as-narrator approach begun in Kangaroo Time and Dr WELI's PhD work.

How it starts — three songs in 2026 / 2027. Why We Smile launches with three songs that carry the beginning of the album's story. The arc follows change and choices — inspired by the changing climate and the natural world we belong to — moving through disconnection, reconnection, and our power to choose differently. Each song uses an animal narrator to bring the question close, and each one is built as a small pro-social-change narrative, particularly around how we belong to nature and how we behave toward nature and one another.

Built with an interdisciplinary team — Dr WELI (writer and performer), WildWooHoo Studio (production and co-publishing), and producers, artists and vocalists drawn from the studio's creative register. Where the songs allow, releases are paired with conservation-partner organisations — a long-arc collaborative community building alongside the album.

Status: Songwriting in progress · Phase 1 recording July 2026 · lead single Q3 2026. We welcome label partners, grant co-applicants, conservation-partner organisations, music-video co-financing, and screening commissioners for this release. Creative collaborators join through our creative register.

Project 02 · Previous Major · Released 2024 · Living catalogue

Kangaroo Time

Song + music video + dance · Science Magazine's Dance Your PhD International Winner 2024 · 238+ media features

wildwoohoo.com/projects/#kangaroo-time

Concept developed: 2022–2023 · Released: February 2024 · © Weliton Menário Costa / WildWooHoo. All rights reserved.

Concept, credits & press

What it is. A massive interdisciplinary undertaking — original song, full music video, embodied choreography, and a diverse Canberra dance cast — that translated Dr WELI's PhD research on individual personality in 300+ eastern grey kangaroos into a high-energy, multicultural pop-cultural object. Music, film, dance, science, all in one piece.

The work. The song parallels kangaroo behavioural differences with the varying dance styles and aesthetics of the micro-communities living in Canberra, Australia's capital — touching on diversity, individuality, and how groups make space for difference. Differences lead to diversity became the project's core message and the lens for everything WildWooHoo has built since.

Achievements — the project that proved it works.

  • Winner, Science Magazine's Dance Your PhD 2024 — the international competition for translating PhD research into dance.
  • 238+ media features worldwide — Science, NPR, Smithsonian, The Guardian, BBC, ABC, O Globo, Triple J, and more.
  • 155K+ video views · used by schools and researchers internationally.
  • Recognised as a highlight of ANU's international engagement by former ANU Vice-Chancellor Professor Genevieve Bell — one of the wider-reaching news materials coming from ANU.
  • Overwhelming positive public response — from RN Breakfast in Australia to MSNBC in the United States to thousands of viewer comments expressing genuine emotional response.

Why it matters now. Kangaroo Time is the project that proved a serious science-music synthesis can win at international scale and reach genuine mainstream audiences. It is the precedent that makes Why We Smile credible — and the prototype for WildWooHoo's interconnected narratives approach (every WildWooHoo song uses a non-human narrator as proximity-bridge to a social-change theme).

Source: Dr WELI's PhD research on kangaroo personality, social environment, and maternal effects (ANU). Peer-reviewed paper ↗

The Photography Project

Ongoing photography sessions · Art-science-community open-call · Co-create human-animal imagery

wildwoohoo.com/projects/#photography-sessions → Collaborate with us

First session: Canberra / Gungahlin, February 2021 · Status: Open for new sessions in other locations · © Weliton Menário Costa / WildWooHoo. All rights reserved.

Concept, sessions & how to co-create

Co-create photography that connects human-animal societies.

What it is. A recurring, location-based creative project for photographers, models, hair-and-make-up artists, stylists, and creatives who want to collaborate on imagery that explores the connection between humans, animals, and the landscapes we share. Each session is a co-creation: WildWooHoo provides the concept and direction (grounded in Dr WELI's behavioural-ecology research); participating photographers and models bring craft and presence.

First session. Canberra / Gungahlin, February 2021 — the staged photography series translating Dr WELI's PhD findings on kangaroo personality, social environment, and maternal effects into a human portrait series. Each model stands in for a finding, an axis of personality, a social role, a behavioural pattern. The photographs from that first session sit across the WildWooHoo site as the brand's photographic memory — on the home page showreel, the music page, the educational page, and the impact page.

Open for new sessions — in other cities, towns, landscapes.

The first session was in Canberra. The next can be anywhere there's a community of photographers + models + creatives who want to co-host a session in their own location. Each new session adds to a growing visual catalogue at the intersection of art, ecology, and community.

What participants get.

  • Modelling and portfolio material at the intersection of art, ecology, and community
  • Co-credit on the published work, on this site and across published outputs
  • Participation in WildWooHoo's broader art-science social-engagement community

What WildWooHoo gets.

  • Visual material that builds out the studio's interconnected-narratives portfolio
  • Community connection in new locations
  • Informal science communication — every session produces material that helps science narratives reach audiences who don't normally encounter them

Strengths of the format. Modelling portfolio + art-science social engagement + community building + informal science communication — a fun, creative project involving creatives, community, and science.

Status: Ongoing. The first session is released and visible across this site. Open to co-host a session in your community.

Project 04 · In development · Open to collaborate

Wild Work Sessions

Fieldwork · wildlife documentation · the source the studio's work grows from

wildwoohoo.com/projects/#wild-work-sessions → Collaborate with us

Stage: In development · Reach: Australia first, then wider · © Weliton Menário Costa / WildWooHoo. All rights reserved.

The concept & how to collaborate

The work starts in the field.

What it is. Kangaroo Time came from years living alongside real kangaroos. Wild Work Sessions makes that the method: go where wild animals are, live the moment with them, and bring it home as music, movement, image and sound. Dr WELI embeds with field scientists on their research, then turns the lived experience of the animal into creative work.

Each session gathers photography, film and field recordings that feed the studio's songs, films and learning programs — and stands alone as wildlife storytelling in its own right. Grounded in real behavioural ecology, made to be felt.

Built with field scientists, not just about them.

This is made with behavioural ecologists and PhD researchers. Their fieldwork gets cultural reach and documentation; the studio gets the lived truth of the animal. Two-way, every time. Australia first, then the wider world the science reaches, including Africa and Brazil.

Open to collaborate. We work with wildlife cinematographers, field-sound recordists, and field scientists on these sessions. If that is you — or you have upcoming fieldwork that would welcome a few days of creative company — we would love to hear from you.
