Living Wild, where the studio's work begins in the field

WildWooHoo Studio · June 2026

Living Wild is the field strand WildWooHoo is opening, where the creative process starts among wild animals, built together with the field scientists already studying them, so the studio can live the moment alongside the animal and bring it home as music, movement, image and sound.

Field

The creative process starts in the field. Living Wild sends the studio to where wild animals are, to live the moment alongside them, watch how they behave, and carry that lived experience home as music, movement, image and sound. This is the method Kangaroo Time already came from, when Dr WELI spent years doing behavioural-ecology fieldwork with wild kangaroos, and Living Wild makes that way of working deliberate, so the studio goes to the animal first and lets everything else grow from the time spent there.

What the field gives is the lived truth of the animal: the place, the light, the sound of it, the way a social group moves and holds together. A song, a film, a photograph and a field recording all read better when they come from a real morning with a real animal, and that is the source Living Wild is built to gather.

Together

Living Wild is built with field scientists, two-way every time. The studio embeds with behavioural ecologists and PhD researchers on the animal fieldwork they are already doing, in Australia first and then across the wider world the science reaches, working through Dr WELI and his behavioural-ecology network at ANU. The science gains cultural reach and documentation, and the studio gains the lived truth of the animal, so each side comes home with something the other made possible.

A few days of creative company sits lightly alongside a research trip, and it leaves behind a record of the work and a way for more people to meet it. This is the science-participatory heart of the strand, and it is the part the studio most wants to grow.

Animals

The focus is social mammals, and it opens out to all social animals. The creative throughline is sci pop and the parallels it draws between animal lives and human ones, the comparative cognition that lets one species light up another, and the cultural traditions and imaginaries an animal already carries in the human mind. An animal is a creature in the grass and a figure in a story at once, and Living Wild works both at the same time.

This is where the field and the song meet. The lived experience of a social animal becomes source material for sci pop and for EVER+GROW, the album WildWooHoo is building, and it carries the studio's warm living-world lens, the field scientist in the grass beside the animal under study.

Document

Each visit gathers nature photography, wildlife film, writing and field sound recording. That material becomes narratives and audiovisuals, source material for the music, and standalone wildlife storytelling that stands on its own. The same morning with an animal can feed a song, a short film and a photograph, and each form keeps its own life.

Living Wild also goes to heritage places, so the work stays close to culture as well as to the living world. A place that holds human meaning and a place that holds wild animals are often the same place, and the strand is made to honour both at once.

Welcome

Living Wild is a concept in active development, and it is an open invitation. The studio welcomes research labs running animal fieldwork in Australia and around the world, university partnerships, and institutions working in media and wildlife documentary film, to spend time together and co-create. Living Wild is the door, and it is open from today.

The studio also welcomes craft collaborators for the field, named here so the right people can recognise themselves.

  • A wildlife cinematographer or director of photography, to film the animal and the place alongside the studio.
  • A field-sound recordist, to capture the ambient and species sound that feeds the music and the films.
  • Field scientists and PhD researchers with upcoming animal fieldwork who would welcome a few days of creative company.

Roots

Living Wild grows from work that is already real. Kangaroo Time came from Dr WELI's doctoral fieldwork on the personality and social behaviour of wild eastern grey kangaroos, and it became a song, a film and a dance cast that travelled widely. Living Wild takes the lesson of that work, that the strongest creative material comes from real time spent with the animal, and it builds a repeatable way to go and get it.

Dr WELI is a behavioural ecologist (PhD, ANU) and a performing artist, and Living Wild is where those two lives do the same work. It is a strand the studio is opening now, in the open, with room for the scientists, the film makers and the institutions who want to build it together.

Join

If you run a research lab, a university programme or a media and wildlife-film institution, and there is animal fieldwork ahead where a few days of creative company would be welcome, the studio would love to hear from you. The same goes for a wildlife cinematographer or a field-sound recordist who wants to spend time in the field and co-create. Write to hello@wildwoohoo.com, and let us go and live wild together.
