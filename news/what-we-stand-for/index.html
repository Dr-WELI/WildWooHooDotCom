What guides us · 25 June 2026

What we stand for, the commitments that guide the work

WildWooHoo Studio · 25 June 2026

When science travels into a song it arrives as feeling and image first, and the science rides home inside it, and that simple move is the thing WildWooHoo studio cares about most and the reason for the six commitments set out below, drawn from the work we have actually made and honest about the parts that are still openings, marked as aims where they are openings.

WildWooHoo is a studio for science in culture. We coin and build sci pop, a science aesthetic and theme in contemporary popular music, and we make it through one repeatable, teachable craft that founder Dr Weliton Menario Costa (WELI), behavioural ecologist (PhD, ANU), named the interconnected narrative. The heart of that craft is the polysemic element, a single phrase, sound or image that reads true inside a scientific story and a human story at once, so a listener meets the music as feeling and the science arrives through it. Two songs proved the craft, Kangaroo Time and Cabra da Peste, and the album EVER+GROW is where its sound is being built now. This article sets out the six commitments that guide that work. Some are demonstrated in songs we have released. Some are openings the form makes possible, and we mark those as aims, because the honest thing is to be clear about which is which.

Participation, co-creation, collaboration and community

The organising purpose of sci pop is to widen who takes part in popular music and to widen the popular idea of what science and a scientist are. The way we do that is structural. We name a category, we write the method down so anyone can run it, and we set out three honest routes by which real research can reach a song: a scientist who writes from their own work, a science consultant who advises an artist for accuracy while another person writes and sings, and an artist who works straight from the published record.

Kangaroo Time is the demonstrated case of a scientist taking part on his own terms. It is built from WELI's peer-reviewed doctoral research on wild eastern grey kangaroos, it reached Science and academic audiences, and it was named AAAS Dance Your PhD Global Winner 2024. Participation also runs to who counts as a scientist in the popular eye, and here we open the inherited lab-coat picture out to the field scientist, the biologist and the ecologist, with WELI the behavioural ecologist in the grass beside the wild kangaroo as the worked example. What one song demonstrates, the form sets out to make ordinary for many. That wider participation is the aim, supplied with a method and a model so it can happen.

The studio is the collaborative home. WildWooHoo gathers the artists, the work and the consultancy, and it is building EVER+GROW end to end. The method is itself a collaboration of two disciplines inside one writer, behavioural ecology and pop song-craft, written down precisely so the two roles can be shared across two people. Some collaborations are already on the record, including ideas developed with Anna-Sophie Jurgens that underlie how we place science inside a song. The wider collaborative reach, artists and scientists and consultants working together across fields and languages, is the opening the open method and the studio are built to invite.

Inclusion, equity, accessibility, diversity and representation

The membership is open by design. Scientist-artists sit at the core, and the door stays open to any artist drawn to the science, the sound, the aesthetic and the themes, with a science-consultancy layer behind the work. WELI weighed the risk that a scientist-only genre would draw the firmest boundary while also drawing a line of division, and he chose an open genre. The three routes make that inclusion concrete, because a songwriter with no laboratory can make true sci pop by going to the science itself. The guard rail that keeps it honest is provenance: every sci pop work holds real, traceable research behind the song.

Equity here is an opening we name as an aim. In cinema the science consultant is a settled, paid, theorised role. In music the same contribution has appeared as celebrated one-offs, a guest brought in once and remarked upon. We name the musical science consultant as a standing role, give it a home and a reason to recur, and state the aim plainly, to make that recurrence ordinary and payable. How those rewards come to be shared is still ahead of us.

Accessibility is built into the craft. The polysemic element lets two listeners take two true and compatible meanings from one surface, so the science arrives through the music itself, carried inside what a listener already enjoys. The song carries the hook and the video carries the heavier research detail, so the hook earns attention and the video spends it on the science. Diversity shows in the range of sciences a song can carry and in who counts as a scientist in the popular picture, the field ecologist standing beside the lab figure. Representation follows the same move, widening the inherited image of science out to the researcher in the grass, the animal under study and the open landscape as a place of knowledge, and our bilingual English and Portuguese practice broadens that representation across more than one language and culture.

Culture, identity, belonging and context

We treat science as a cultural system, a thing carried in stories, images and meanings, so the science reaches a listener through culture and as feeling first. The clearest sign of this is that each song is built from a cultural frame the listener already lives inside. Cabra da Peste sets its whole goat-behaviour science inside Brazilian Carnival, because February is at once the goat's oestrus and mating season in the biology and Carnival, Brazil's February festival, in the culture, so the breeding-season hierarchy of a goat herd is sung as a Carnival song about rank and desire. The song is in Portuguese and runs the craft natively in Brazilian Portuguese, where "cabra" names the female goat of the research and a tough, charismatic man of everyday speech at once. Kangaroo Time works the same way through Australian culture, where "mates" is a cultural word as much as a behavioural-ecology term.

Identity lives in the same place. The title phrase of Kangaroo Time carries WELI's life across three readings at once: his time researching kangaroos, his time as a PhD student, and his time living in Australia as a migrant. A specialist, a fellow researcher and a fellow migrant each find themselves in one surface. Belonging is there in the line "you surrounded by mates, are you feeling alright?", which holds kangaroo sociality, the very social structure the research measured, alongside human peer pressure, belonging and vulnerability, so it asks a question about fitting in that is true of a kangaroo group and of a person at once. Context is what makes every double work, because "mates" carries its two meanings only in Australian English and "cabra" carries its two only in Brazilian Portuguese, and the method honours that by mapping each song to its own setting.

One commitment here we hold as an opening, and we name it as an aim. Sci pop's home material, the living world, deep time, and the relation between people, other creatures and the places they live, runs close to domains where Indigenous knowledges hold deep authority. The open membership makes room for Indigenous knowledge-holders to take part on their own terms, through the routes we already name. That is a relationship to enter with consent and authority, led by knowledge-holders themselves, a relationship to build. The honest verb today is opening.

Emotion, resonance, curiosity, wonder and memory

Sci pop carries science as feeling first, by design. The research is embedded inside familiar cultural stories and relationships, so a listener meets a finding through an affective experience before meeting it as content. The mates line lands as a tender question about belonging while it names the kangaroo social group the research measured, so the emotion is the carrier and the behavioural ecology rides inside it. In Cabra da Peste the goat herd's breeding-season dominance hierarchy is sung as a Carnival song about heat and desire, so the social charge of February carries the science of oestrus and pheromonal signalling.

Resonance comes from the polysemic element, the single surface two listeners read two true ways, which is what lets one line reach a specialist, a peer and a stranger at once and have each take something real. Curiosity is named as a shared value of the community and built into the craft, the song opening the door with feeling and movement, the video carrying the research into reach. That is the route by which Kangaroo Time travelled to Science and academic audiences. Wonder is one of our stated themes and one of EVER+GROW's organising ideas, and it is sourced in the living world the research describes, then carried as motif into accessible song. Memory follows from holding the work to pop song-form, hook-led and built to move a room, with a double meaning that lets the science travel home inside something a listener already remembers. The fuller emotional reach at album scale, where this affect becomes a sustained pop statement, is what EVER+GROW is being built to carry, and that work is in active construction.

Translation, storytelling, meaning-making and multilingualism

Translation is the core problem we exist to solve, how a peer-reviewed finding becomes a song a non-specialist meets as music first. The mechanism we name is nature-centred translation, drawn from the worked Kangaroo Time case, where the doctoral research reaches the listener through kangaroos as creatures and through human stories of belonging and difference, carried by movement, character and feeling. Storytelling is the second step of the method, growing two parallel stories from a single theme, a scientific one and a socio-cultural one, tied together through shared place, tone, community and character. Cabra da Peste shows storytelling at full saturation, an entire account of oestrus, pheromonal signalling and dominance delivered as one continuous Carnival courtship story.

Meaning-making is how science travels, so the meaning is the medium through which the science is met, and the polysemic element is the engineered instance of it. EVER+GROW scales the device to a whole record. It is built on indeterminate growth, the way some animals keep growing through life, legible to a behavioural ecologist as the album's organising biological idea and to any listener as a promise about growth, persistence and becoming. Multilingualism is in the work from the origin, because the craft is proven native to two languages, English in Kangaroo Time and Brazilian Portuguese in Cabra da Peste, with the doubles freshly native to each tongue. WELI's own bilingual life as a Brazilian-Australian is the lived ground for this. Because the method depends on a language's own everyday idiom, it opens room for sci pop works in further tongues, an opening the form holds, with the demonstrated cases being English and Brazilian Portuguese.

Impact, engagement, learning and how science and society grow together

Impact runs through the method, the polysemic element letting a listener take in the work as feeling and meet the science through it. Kangaroo Time shows the mechanism working, and the concrete marker we record for it is AAAS Dance Your PhD Global Winner 2024, a named award and a reception that reached Science and academic audiences. The wider impact we aim at, science carried into the popular song for general listeners, is what EVER+GROW is being built to demonstrate inside the contemporary music industry.

We hold learning lightly and on purpose. A sci pop song works as pop first, and the learning is the science a listener can meet through the work, an experience to absorb in passing, kept accurate by the science-consultancy layer. The aim of WELI's artist-researcher practice is music that fosters appreciation of nature, a sense of belonging, and reflection on diversity, on how we relate to one another, and on how we relate to other creatures. That is the register we hold, opening room for appreciation, belonging and reflection, with any measured shift still to be shown.

The legacy we are building is a place for science and for scientists in popular music, a place meant to outlast any one record. It rests on a named, repeatable role, the musical science consultant, and on a method anyone can run. EVER+GROW releases as a growing album, its songs landing as singles into one persistent digital album that expands with every addition, so the record grows across months and the body of work gathers in one place. Benevolence is its Phase-1 EP, the opening movement. This is how we think science and society grow together: by giving researchers who are also makers a name to work under, a method to work by, and a body of work to build on, so the field widens with each person who joins. The studio holds these commitments now, in public and on the record, and builds the rest in the open.
