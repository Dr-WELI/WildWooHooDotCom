Falling Walls Engage 2025, Top 30 worldwide

WildWooHoo Studio · for Science and the People · updated 14 June 2026

In 2025, WildWooHoo Studio was named in the global Top 30 of the Falling Walls Engage prize, an annual recognition by the Falling Walls Foundation for projects that engage broad publics with research-grounded ideas. The studio sits in the Science and the People category.

What Falling Walls Engage is

The Falling Walls Foundation runs an annual prize for science engagement, complementary to its long-running Science Summit in Berlin. The Engage prize asks a specific question: which projects, in the past year, have most effectively connected publics with research? A jury of artists, scientists, and engagement practitioners evaluates submissions worldwide. The Top 30 cohort is presented at the annual Berlin event.

The published scoring rubric

The published evaluation criteria are explicit and weighted. They have since shaped how WildWooHoo frames every funder-facing piece.

40%Impact, sustained measurable improvements
30%Innovation, unique forward-thinking approach
20%Approach, inclusive participatory co-creation
10%Overall Impression, clarity and pitch quality

The rubric is on the public Falling Walls page; this is not internal interpretation. Every R&E grant brief, EPK section, and partner pitch now references back to it. Impact comes first, weighted heaviest. Artist biography comes last, weighted lightest. Funder-facing copy under the studio's name follows that ordering verbatim.

What WildWooHoo's entry argued

The studio's case rested on the work that had landed in the year prior: Kangaroo Time (AAAS Dance Your PhD Global Winner 2024), the verified media circulation of 238+ features across five continents (Isentia, 2024), the catalysed downstream engagements (Encontro Anual de Etologia 2024 plenary, ACT National Science Week 2024 and 2025, public talks and workshops), and the studio formation itself as the structural answer to "what next." The argument was structural: behavioural-ecology-to-pop-cultural-output is the studio's signature method, not a one-off success.

What the rubric ruled in (and out)

The 40/30/20/10 weighting has practical consequences for how the studio writes:

  • Impact, 40%. Named outcomes with specific numbers, dates, and partners. "Three schools reached" beats "many schools." "12 paid workshops delivered" beats "engagement growing." Sentiment data (98.2% positive on YouTube) supplements but never replaces concrete outcomes.
  • Innovation, 30%. Methodological novelty, named. The intersection of behavioural ecology, queer creative practice, multilingual delivery, and pop-cultural audiovisual production is the signature. The studio's role is to keep that intersection productive, not to chase trends.
  • Approach, 20%. Participatory and co-creation design, named. The Canberra dance cast's twelve dance styles came from communities, not from professional choreography. Future WildWooHoo programming follows the same casting principle: design with the communities that are about to be represented.
  • Overall Impression, 10%. Clarity and pitch quality. The studio's anti-AI-tell discipline (plain language, named partners, concrete deliverables, no hedging) covers this slot at the editorial level.

What this changed for the studio

Three concrete shifts that followed the recognition:

  • Funder-facing copy now references the rubric directly. Every grant brief begins by naming a specific Impact outcome (sustained, measurable), then names the Innovation angle, then names the Approach mechanism, then closes with the Overall pitch. Artist biography goes last.
  • The studio's own measurement loop tightened. YouTube analytics (Menário Costa 2026) are now retrieved and reported with the same discipline as press circulation. The 98.2% positive sentiment ratio, the 64.5% off-platform referral share, the 2:19 average view duration, all feed funder-facing impact statements.
  • The studio formation itself became citable. Founding a multidisciplinary creative business as the downstream answer to a research-to-creative project is now part of how the studio describes its own theory of change. Documented at this URL, updated as the work grows.
