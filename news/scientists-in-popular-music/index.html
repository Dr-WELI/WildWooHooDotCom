A new path, scientists in popular music and culture

WildWooHoo Studio · 25 June 2026

When a scientist who has spent years on a finding picks up a song, something opens that a journal article cannot offer, a place where peer-reviewed research reaches a wide public as feeling and image first, and where the scientist becomes an artist with a body of music to build on.

This is the story of how Dr WELI, a behavioural ecologist (PhD, ANU), became an artist, and of the path that journey now opens for other scientists. It starts in the field, with wild kangaroos and a research question, and it grows into a song, a genre and an album. Along the way it names a fresh kind of work in popular music and culture, and a joyful career direction for graduates who love both the science and the song.

From the field to the song

WELI is a behavioural ecologist who studies animal behaviour through an evolutionary and ecological lens. His peer-reviewed doctoral research at the Australian National University looked at the personality and social behaviour of wild eastern grey kangaroos, across individual personality, the social environment an animal lives in, and maternal effects passed from one generation to the next. The fieldwork ran at Wilsons Promontory in Victoria, and the findings are published in Behavioral Ecology.

That research became Kangaroo Time, a song and a film that carry the science as theme and image. The work was named the AAAS Dance Your PhD Global Winner 2024, and it reached audiences well beyond the original paper. Its title phrase holds three true meanings at once. Kangaroo time is WELI's time researching kangaroos, his time as a PhD student, and his time living in Australia as a migrant. The lyric line "you surrounded by mates, are you feeling alright?" carries the same doubling, because in Australian English mates names both a kangaroo social group and a circle of human friends, so the line holds kangaroo sociality, the very social structure the research measured, alongside human belonging. A specialist, a fellow researcher and a fellow migrant each take a true reading from one surface.

A second song followed the same craft in another language. Cabra da Peste grows from WELI's earlier undergraduate fieldwork on goat social behaviour, the mating-season dominance hierarchy and the signals that organise it, and it was commissioned for and performed at the Etologia 2024 plenary in Brazil, on the theme of creativity and science unifying communities. Sung in Portuguese, it runs nearly every key term as a double that works in Brazilian Portuguese, where February carries both the goat's mating season and Carnival, and where cabra names both the female goat of the research and a tough, charismatic man. Two sciences, two languages, one method.

What the scientist brings, and what it meets

WELI's research interests reach across comparative cognition, evolution, and the cultural imaginaries of science and scientists, the images a culture forms of what science is and who does it. These interests meet popular culture directly. A scientist arrives with real findings, with the habit of asking honest questions, and with an expanded picture of who counts as a scientist, the field ecologist in the grass beside the lab figure at the bench. Popular music and film are where a culture already pictures science, and they have welcomed that picture mostly as image and theme. The scientist who makes songs can carry genuine research into that space and widen the picture from the inside.

The scholarship supports the opening. Science has been studied closely on screen, where David Kirby described the science consultant who shapes how research appears in film, and where science is carried "as a genre, theme, or conventional representation in fiction." Popular music has drawn a far thinner shelf, and the question of how a real finding becomes a popular song sits largely open. That openness is room to work.

Sci pop, science as theme in hook-led pop

From those two songs grew sci pop, the genre WildWooHoo coins and builds. Sci pop is a science aesthetic and theme in contemporary popular music, a name for hook-led pop songs that carry science as theme and imagery, in the lyric and in the sound. It ranges across science, from peer-reviewed research at one pole to the cultural imaginaries of science and scientists at the other, and across nature and its processes and humans and their technologies. The science rides inside the song as image and double meaning, so a listener meets the music as feeling first and the science arrives through it.

Sci pop carries three grounded dimensions. The aesthetic is the cultural imagery of science and of scientists, widened from the lab-coat physicist to the field scientist, the biologist and the ecologist in the natural world. The theme is the transit across science, from a song grown straight from a finding to a song that reaches for a robot, a rocket or a deep future. The community is the people who make it, scientist-artists at the core, open to any artist drawn to the same material, with a science-consultancy layer that keeps the science accurate. Sci pop sits in the lineage of artist coinages such as Burna Boy's Afro-fusion and Kraftwerk's robot pop, names that mark a way of working ahead of any catalogue. The full account of the genre, its peers and its method lives on the Sci pop page.

EVER+GROW, the album that sets the sound

EVER+GROW is the album WildWooHoo is building end to end out of science, technology and nature, and it sets sci pop's sound, look and place inside the contemporary music industry. It is conceived whole from the same premise, with the concept, the lyrics, the composition and the release strategy each grown from that source. Its organising idea is indeterminate growth, the way some animals keep growing through life, which the album reads at once as an image from the natural world and as a figure for a human life that keeps becoming. WELI releases its songs as singles over time into one persistent, growing album, so the record expands month by month, and the growth-over-time of the body of work enacts the biological idea as structure. Benevolence is its Phase-1 EP, the first release and the opening movement.

A path this names for scientists

Here the story opens outward. One artist's journey names a path that many can take. Sci pop gives the researcher who makes songs a recognised kind of work, a method to work by, and a name to be cited under, so a scientist who wants their research to live as music now has somewhere it belongs. It also names a second role, the musical science consultant, the scientist who would rather advise than perform, lending a song the accuracy and authority a scientist lends a film while another person writes and sings. Both roles are real and workable, and sci pop names them so they can recur.

For graduates this is an exciting and grounded career direction. A science training can lead into popular-culture science consultancy, advising on music, film and media so that the science they carry is true, and it can lead into the studio as an artist, working honestly from the published record. It widens who takes part in popular music and culture, and it widens the popular idea of what science and a scientist are, the field ecologist beside the lab figure, the social life of a goat herd and the personality of a wild kangaroo beside the spaceship and the circuit board.

An invitation to build it with us

WildWooHoo builds this work in the open, and it is a warm invitation. We welcome partners and funders who want to back the path, schools, festivals, broadcasters, publishers, universities, and cultural and research funders who value work that carries genuine science into the songs and screens people already love.
