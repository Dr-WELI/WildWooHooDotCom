The studio method

From complex idea to creative experience

We begin with something true and difficult to carry. Then we find the human entry point, choose the right creative form and build a way for people to enter it.

The same method supports commissioned work and WildWooHoo originals. The brief changes; the movement from knowledge to culture does not.

See the five moves

What begins the work

A research finding, a landscape, a strategy, a lived experience or a question that matters—but has not yet found the form in which people will choose to meet it.

What the method protects

  • The truth of the source. The science or knowledge remains structurally meaningful, not decorative.
  • The freedom of the form. The song, film, story or live moment must work as culture, not merely explanation.
  • The people inside it. Co-creators and audiences are participants, not props or passive recipients.
  • The route outward. The intended audience and how the work will reach them are designed from the beginning.

Five moves

  1. Source. Identify the real knowledge, material or question worth carrying.
  2. Entry point. Find where that source touches emotion, identity, memory, humour, desire or everyday life.
  3. Form. Choose the medium that can carry both the source and the feeling: music, film, story, image, movement or a combination.
  4. Participation. Build with the people represented in the work and create a meaningful way for audiences to enter it.
  5. Release. End with one clear idea and a practical route into public culture.

Where it came from

The method grew through Dr WELI's movement from evolutionary ecology into recording and performance. Kangaroo Time carried his ANU research on kangaroo personality into a pop song, film and multicultural dance work, becoming the AAAS Dance Your PhD Overall Winner 2024. Sci pop now names the wider music practice: science as aesthetic, theme or method in contemporary pop.

Where we use it

Commissioned music and film concepts. Research-to-culture projects. WildWooHoo originals. Public engagement and science-art development. Dr WELI's keynotes, workshops and participatory programs use a live version of the same sequence.
